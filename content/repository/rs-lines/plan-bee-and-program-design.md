Establishing a line is easy; maintaining one without painting yourself into a genetic corner is the hard part. The two documents here are the program-design references AQBBA leans on.

## The documents

The **Plan Bee Breeding Manual** (2nd edition) comes out of the national honey bee genetic improvement program. It is the most directly applicable manual for Australian conditions: trait recording, breeding values, and program structure, written for working queen breeders rather than geneticists. Members contributing data to the VSH program will recognise most of its recording conventions — ours are deliberately compatible.

**Initiation and Implementation of Honey Bee Breeding Programs** (Uzunov, Brascamp, Du & Büchler) is the COLOSS guidance on starting and running a program: prioritising traits, structuring selection, and — pointedly relevant to us — the emphasis on local adaptation and varroa resistance as first-class breeding objectives.

## The trap this sub-topic exists to avoid

Both documents circle the same warning from different directions: a line maintained too narrowly gains trait expression and loses vigour. The program's live example is discussed candidly in the forum — the Coalvale 17 population, eight generations closed, with brood viability sliding — and the outcross recovery trial (PRJ-02) is the response. Read the documents, then the forum thread, and the abstract risk becomes concrete.
