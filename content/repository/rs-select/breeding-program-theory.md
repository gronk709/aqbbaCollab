Before arguing about which colony to breed from, it pays to agree on how a breeding program is actually supposed to work. The two papers attached here are the program's theoretical foundation.

## The documents

**The Basic Concept of Honey Bee Breeding Programs** (Uzunov, Brascamp & Büchler) explains the fundamentals: defining breeding objectives, performance testing, and evaluating characteristics for genetic improvement. If you read one theory paper as an AQBBA member, read this one — the program's assessment protocols and the annual line performance table are both built on the framework it describes.

**Breeding for improved fertility of honey bees** (Frost, Chapman, Banks & Hermesch) is Australian work reviewing fertility traits in queens and drones, breeding values, and practical recommendations for improving reproductive performance. Fertility is the trait everyone forgets until a season's worth of poorly-mated queens reminds them.

## Reading these as a member

The papers give you the *why* behind things the program asks of you: why every assessment uses the same protocol (comparability is what makes breeding values possible), why colonies under treatment are excluded from selection data, and why two seasons of assessment are required before a line appears in the performance table.
