Selection without mating control is a slow way of going nowhere: you can pick the best queen mothers every season, but if their daughters mate with whatever drones happen to be flying, half of every generation's genetics is out of your hands.

## The documents here

The **COLOSS mating control paper** (Uzunov, Brascamp, Du & Büchler) makes the scientific case: why controlled mating is critical for holding genetic gains across generations, and what the realistic strategies are — geographic isolation, drone saturation, and instrumental insemination.

The **drone mother colonies note** covers the practical arithmetic of drone saturation at member scale: how many drone mother colonies you need, and where to position them relative to the mating yard so your drones dominate the congregation areas your virgins actually reach.

## How this connects to the rest of the program

Mating control is the mechanism that makes everything in the Selection and breeding value sub-topic effective. For members without access to an isolated site, drone saturation is the accessible middle path — no remote island required, just deliberate drone management. For the closed-population work in the program, instrumental insemination takes over; see the Instrumental insemination sub-topic.
