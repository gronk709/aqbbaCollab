Instrumental insemination is the only way to fully control both sides of a mating, which is why the program's closed-population work depends on it. It is also equipment-heavy, technique-sensitive, and unforgiving of shortcuts — so the attached **standard methods paper for instrumental insemination** is the reference this sub-topic is anchored on.

## What it covers

Equipment and setup, anaesthesia (CO₂ timing and its effect on onset of laying), semen collection and storage, the insemination procedure itself, and post-insemination queen care. The sections on semen quality assessment are particularly worth close reading — poor semen handling is the silent failure mode that gets misdiagnosed as bad technique.

## Before you invest

II earns its considerable cost when you need certainty of parentage: closed populations, single-drone inseminations for research, or preserving a line whose drones can't be saturated locally. For most members running open selection, good drone saturation (see Mating yard design) delivers most of the benefit at a fraction of the setup. Talk to Hélène Marchetti — the program's II specialist — before buying a station; her equipment recommendations have saved several members from expensive false starts, and semen from the program's closed population is available by arrangement.
